## The problem

People coordinate animal rescue through social networks and messaging groups. Requests can lose their original source, location, or current status. Rescuers must decide whether information is reliable while facing time pressure and limited resources.

The study asked how rescuers find, validate, and share information about animals at risk. It explored the information barriers that affect rescue decisions and coordination.

## Approach

- Defined the research domain, participant profile, and recruitment criteria.
- Tried an initial screening form, then used referrals and direct contacts after scheduling proved difficult.
- Wrote an interview script later revised after the pilot interview.
- Conducted four main semi-structured interviews (dynamic script), identified in the analysis as E1–E4.
- Compared participant goals, obstacles, workarounds, and supporting statements.
- Identified recurring patterns, disagreements and unexpected behaviors.
- Built two evidence-based personas and associated problem scenarios.

## Findings

| Finding | Design opportunity |
| --- | --- |
| Location information becomes stale before a rescuer arrives. | Make location and case updates explicit. |
| Uncertain sources and suspected fraud reduce trust. | Show information origin and evidence of case progress. |
| Rescuers depend on informal networks for resources and support. | Help people coordinate support around each animal. |
| Care knowledge often comes from experience rather than clear guidance. | Provide guidance for people with less rescue experience. |
| Failed adoptions create additional work and emotional strain. | Support careful adopter screening and follow-up. |

These opportunities are interpretations of the research findings. They are not validated product outcomes.
